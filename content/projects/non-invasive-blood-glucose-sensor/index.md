## Overview

This collaborative undergraduate project investigated a low-cost infrared optical sensing concept for glucose concentration. It is an educational instrumentation prototype, not a medical or diagnostic device.

## Engineering problem

The experiment asks whether received infrared-light intensity changes measurably with glucose concentration under controlled benchtop conditions.

## Approach and implementation

The documented setup combines an infrared transmitter, prepared glucose solution, photodetector, bridge measurement circuit, low-pass filtering, an Arduino Nano and an LCD. Tests used prepared solutions of approximately 50–500 mg/dm³; no human blood measurements or human trials were performed.

## Outcome and limitations

The experiments showed observable sensor-response changes, but between-session variation exposed limitations in reliability and reproducibility. The prototype has no clinical validation, diagnostic accuracy claim, regulatory approval or suitability for medical decisions.

## Technologies and methods

- Infrared LED and photodetection
- Bridge measurement circuit
- Low-pass filtering
- Arduino Nano instrumentation
- Controlled prepared-solution experiments
